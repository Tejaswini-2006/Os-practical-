5. Write-up


Aim
To implement disk scheduling algorithms SSTF, SCAN and C-LOOK considering the initial head movement away from the spindle.


Objectives
1. To understand different disk scheduling algorithms.
2. To implement SSTF, SCAN and C-LOOK algorithms.
3. To calculate total head movement.
4. To compare the performance of different disk scheduling algorithms.


Theory
Disk scheduling is an operating system technique used to decide the order in which disk I/O requests are serviced. Its main purpose is to reduce head movement and improve disk access performance.

1. SSTF (Shortest Seek Time First)
- Selects the disk request closest to the current head position.
- Reduces total seek time.
- May cause starvation for requests that are far away.
2. SCAN (Elevator Algorithm)
- The disk head moves in one direction and services requests along the way.
- It reaches the disk boundary and then reverses direction.
- It provides better and more uniform service compared to SSTF in many workloads.
3. C-LOOK (Circular LOOK)
- The disk head moves in one direction and services all requests in that direction.
- After reaching the last pending request, it jumps to the first pending request at the opposite end.
- It does not travel to the physical disk boundary unnecessarily.

Algorithm
1. Start the program.
2. Accept the number of disk requests.
3. Read the disk request queue.
4. Accept the initial head position and total number of cylinders.
5. Sort the disk requests.
6. Apply SSTF by selecting the nearest pending request.
7. Apply SCAN by moving towards higher cylinders, reaching the disk boundary and reversing direction.
8. Apply C-LOOK by servicing higher requests first and then jumping to the lowest pending request.
9. Calculate and display the total head movement for each algorithm.
10. Stop the program.
Conclusion
Thus, SSTF, SCAN and C-LOOK disk scheduling algorithms were successfully implemented. The request sequence and total head movement were calculated for each algorithm, helping to understand and compare their working and performance.
